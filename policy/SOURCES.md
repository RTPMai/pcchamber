# Where this information comes from, and how to check it

As of the September 2026 review the site carries **95 source links across 39 domains**. About half point at official government sites.

This file is the fact-checking guide. Use it when you write a new entry or when a member says something on the page is wrong.

---

## The rule: one primary source, one independent confirmation

Every factual claim should trace to a **primary source**, meaning the government body that actually did the thing. Anything with a number in it, a date, a name, or a dollar amount should also have a **second, independent** source.

Independent means a different organisation, not a different page on the same website. Two Ballotpedia pages is one source. Two Choose Iowa pages is one source.

If you cannot find a second source, either write the claim more loosely or say plainly in the entry that it is unconfirmed. We did that with the Senate District 23 primary result and it was the right call.

## Tier 2: reliable secondary. Good for confirmation and for the why.

- **[Iowa Capital Dispatch](https://iowacapitaldispatch.com/)**. Nonprofit statehouse reporting, and the best single source for why a bill passed. Currently 6 links.
- **[Business Record](https://www.businessrecord.com/)**. Metro business and development reporting that covers Polk City specifically. 3 links.
- **[Iowa Public Radio](https://www.iowapublicradio.org/)**. Statewide policy coverage.
- **[Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Iowa)**. Neutral election reference. **Caution:** its district pages went stale on us and still showed pre-primary candidate lists months after the primary. Never treat Ballotpedia as current on a race without a second check.
- **Des Moines Register, The Gazette, WHO 13, KCRG.** General reporting. Fine for confirmation, weak as a sole source.

## Tier 3: use with a stated bias in mind

- **[NFIB Iowa](https://www.nfib.com/state-news/iowa)** is currently our most-cited non-government source at 7 links. Their legislative recaps are genuinely the best small-business summary of an Iowa session. But NFIB is an advocacy organisation with positions. Use them for **what a bill does**, never for **whether a bill is good**. Anything we take from NFIB should ideally be confirmed against the enrolled bill text.
- **Cook Political Report.** Race ratings are a forecast, not a fact. Always attribute them as their rating.
- **Greater Des Moines Partnership, chamber and trade groups.** They advocate for their members. Same rule as NFIB.

## Do not trust without checking

**Accounting and law firm blog posts.** While building the tax section we found firm blogs giving flatly contradictory figures for the same tax year, including wrong deduction percentages and limits that appear to describe repealed law. These rank high in search results and are frequently wrong or outdated. Never cite one. Go to the IRS.

**AI answers and search summaries.** Same problem, one step further removed.

**Anything undated.** A grant page with no year on it is a trap. Deadlines and dollar amounts move annually.

## Known weak spots as of September 2026

Fix these when you get to them.

**Single-source entries.** These rest on one organisation and need a second:

- `business-court-sf639`. Only NFIB. Confirm against the enrolled bill.
- `child-care-hf2514`. Only Iowa Capital Dispatch.
- `cdl-english-sf2426`. Only Iowa Capital Dispatch.
- `cdbg-funding`. Only Polk County. Lower risk, since the county is the primary source for its own grant.

**Entries with no government source at all:** `business-court-sf639`, `child-care-hf2514`, `cdl-english-sf2426`, `grants-start-here`, `grants-food-and-farm`, `governor-race`, `senate-race`, `commercial-residential-imbalance`.

Several are fine as they are. A race with only Ballotpedia is normal. But every entry describing a **law** should cite the enrolled bill, and three of them do not.

**Two links to the same site is one source.** `tax-equipment-vehicles`, `tax-deductions-basics`, `grants-food-and-farm` and `project-watchlist` each show two sources that are really one.

**Unconfirmed:** the Senate District 23 Republican primary result. Two outlets name Wes Enos as the nominee, but we never saw the vote count. Verify at polkelectionia.gov.

## What actually went wrong, so you know what to watch for

Three errors were caught by spot-checking entries that had already been written and looked fine.

1. **Choose Iowa Value-Added Grant.** The entry listed butchers as a good fit. Meat and dairy processing are explicitly **not eligible** and have separate programs. A member could have wasted weeks.
2. **Manufacturing 4.0.** The dollar figures were right but the entry omitted the required 1 to 1 cash match, the $75,000 lifetime cap, and the fact that the mandatory CIRAS assessment window closes roughly six weeks *before* applications open.
3. **AMP'D Iowa.** An 80% training reimbursement program, open and accepting applications, was missing from the site entirely.

The pattern: **dollar amounts were usually right, eligibility rules and deadlines were usually wrong or missing.** Those are what to check hardest.

## A five-minute check for any new entry

1. Can you point at a `.gov` page that says this?
2. Is there a second source from a different organisation?
3. Does every date and dollar figure appear in at least one primary source?
4. For any grant: who is eligible, who is **excluded**, is a match required, and when does the window actually open and close?
5. Is the source page dated, and is that date recent enough to trust?
6. If the answer to any of these is no, either soften the claim or say in the entry that it is unconfirmed.

Getting it right matters more than getting it out fast. A member who acts on something wrong here is worse off than a member who never read the page.
